<p class="eyebrow">Research note · AME(13,26)</p>
<h1>An absolutely maximally entangled state of thirteen 26-level systems</h1>
{% include page-dates.html %}
<p class="lede">An explicit construction in local dimension 26, combining a Paley graph, a bipartite Cauchy phase, and Hermite interpolation over the field with thirteen elements.</p>
</section>

<article class="prose">

<p>This research note presents an explicit <strong>AME(13,26) state</strong>: a pure state of thirteen parties, each with 26 levels, whose reduction to any six parties is maximally mixed. Each party consists of a qubit and a 13-level system. A coupling between their phases gives a state with equal-magnitude amplitudes and exact cancellations on every balanced bipartition.</p>

<h2>The result</h2>
<div class="family-equations">
\[
\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_{A^c}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
=\frac{I_{26^6}}{26^6},\qquad |A|=6.
\]
</div>
<p>There are \(\binom{13}{6}=1{,}716\) such reductions. Smaller subsets are maximally mixed as well. The construction reaches a non-prime-power local dimension, \(26=2\cdot13\), below the thirteen-party range of the <a href="{{ '/research/ame/coupled-branch-families/' | relative_url }}">coupled-branch families</a>, which require an odd prime power \(q\ge17\) in dimension \(2q\). It cannot be obtained by simply tensoring thirteen-party qubit and 13-level AME states, because AME(13,2) does not exist.</p>

<h2>A mixed binary and finite-field phase</h2>
<p>Label the parties by \(a\in\mathbb F_{13}\). At each party, write the local basis as \(|x_a,y_a\rangle\), with \(x_a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(y_a\in\mathbb F_{13}\). For \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/13}\), the amplitudes are</p>
<div class="family-equations">
\[
\psi(x,y)=26^{-13/2}(-1)^{q_G(x)}
\omega^{q_C(y)+x^{\mathsf T}By}.
\]
</div>
<p>Here \(q_M(v)=\sum_{a\lt b}M_{ab}v_av_b\), with any fixed ordering of the party labels. The binary exponent is evaluated modulo two; the exponent of \(\omega\) is evaluated in \(\mathbb F_{13}\), with binary digits read as zero or one in that field. Every amplitude has modulus \(26^{-13/2}\), and every phase is a sign times a thirteenth root of unity.</p>

<h2>The three ingredients</h2>
<p>Let \(Q=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}\) be the nonzero squares in \(\mathbb F_{13}\), and let \(R=\mathbb F_{13}\setminus Q\), which includes zero.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Binary graph \(G\).</strong> The Paley graph on \(\mathbb F_{13}\): \(G_{ab}=1\) when \(a-b\in Q\), and zero otherwise.</li>
<li><strong>Finite-field graph \(C\).</strong> A bipartite Cauchy graph between \(Q\) and \(R\), with \(C_{ab}=C_{ba}=(a-b)^{-1}\) for \(a\in Q\), \(b\in R\), and zero within each part.</li>
<li><strong>Coupling \(B\).</strong> A closed formula obtained from confluent Cauchy, or Hermite, interpolation. Its columns in \(Q\) and its diagonal are zero. For \(b\in R\) and \(a\ne b\), its entries are</li>
</ul>
<div class="family-equations" style="padding-bottom: 0.75rem;">
\[
B_{ab}=\begin{cases}
3b\,(a-b)^{-2}, & a\in Q,\\
9\,(a-b)^{-1}, & a\in R\setminus\{0\},\\
8\,(0-b)^{-1}, & a=0.
\end{cases}
\]
</div>
<p>All arithmetic in \(C\) and \(B\) is modulo thirteen; division means multiplication by a finite-field inverse. These formulas specify the state without storing its full amplitude tensor.</p>

<h2>Why the reductions are maximally mixed</h2>
<p>Flat amplitudes give the required diagonal entries. For off-diagonal entries, summing over the traced-out finite-field digits gives a linear consistency condition. The remaining binary sum factors into terms that can cancel exactly. The Cauchy structure reduces the exceptional cases to a signed-derivative condition.</p>
<p>For a six-party set \(A\), define \(P_A(z)=\prod_{a\in A}(z-a)\). In the Hermite form, with weights \(s_a=5\) for \(a\ne0\) and \(s_0=-5\), the remaining conditions take the form</p>
<div class="family-equations">
\[
\sum_{a\in A}d_a s_a P_A'(a)\ne0,
\]
</div>
<p>for each nonzero signed vector \(d\in\{0,\pm1\}^A\) whose support lies in the binary kernel, \(G_{A^c,A}|d|=0\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). A local controlled translation relates this Hermite form to the displayed coupling matrix. The note proves the cancellation criterion and verifies the resulting finite set of conditions.</p>

<p>The note reports three independent implementations of the full cancellation check. Further checks reproduce the searched state from the closed formula, detect violations after individual matrix entries are perturbed, and evaluate selected coherences by direct summation with exact root-of-unity counts. These checks distinguish the construction from a candidate supported only by small numerical errors.</p>

<h2>Scope and materials</h2>
<p>The result establishes this thirteen-party, 26-level construction. The bipartite Cauchy reduction does not directly supply the remaining cases \(\operatorname{AME}(13,2q)\) for \(q\in\{5,7,9,11\}\), and the analogous Paley construction at seventeen parties does not work for any choice of its row weights. These are limitations of the methods described in the note.</p>
