<h1>Coupled-branch families of AME states</h1>
{% include page-dates.html %}
<p class="lede">Previously unreported AME families for 13, 15 and 17 parties, obtained by coupling binary and prime-dimensional quantum systems.</p>
</section>

<article class="prose">

<p>This is a short announcement of results described in the draft <em>Building maximally entangled states by coupling two simpler parts: Explicit constructions for 13, 15 and 17 parties</em> (20 October 2026).</p>

<h2>New AME families</h2>
<p>For a prime \(p\), the constructions yield three infinite families:</p>
<div class="family-equations">
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{AME}(13,2p),\quad &p\ge 17,\\
\operatorname{AME}(15,2p),\quad &p\ge 17,\\
\operatorname{AME}(17,2p),\quad &p\ge 67.
\end{aligned}
\]
</div>
<p>The manuscript also gives eight explicit examples, verified in exact arithmetic:</p>
<div class="research-table-wrap">
<table class="research-table">
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Parties</th><th scope="col">Local dimensions</th><th scope="col">Binary branches</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>13</td><td>34, 38, 46</td><td>16</td></tr>
<tr><td>15</td><td>26, 34, 38, 46</td><td>8</td></tr>
<tr><td>17</td><td>134</td><td>64</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>
<p>In particular, \(\operatorname{AME}(15,26)\) is obtained below the general prime threshold for the 15-party family. These thresholds are sufficient for the construction; they are not nonexistence bounds below them.</p>

<h2>Construction in brief</h2>
<p>Each local system is divided into a binary and a prime-dimensional part. Several binary branches have maximally mixed small-party marginals <em>on average</em>, while the prime-dimensional branches carry their labels without revealing them to any allowed subset. Their coherent coupling produces a pure state with maximally mixed marginals on every subset of at most half the parties.</p>
<p>The finite examples are supported by exact checks of the stabilizer ingredients and of the prime-dimensional parent states. The infinite families follow from a Cauchy-matrix construction, not from extrapolating numerical examples.</p>

<h2>What is new</h2>
<p><strong>To our knowledge, these particular AME families have not previously been reported.</strong> The underlying coupled-branch principle is established, and the classical code ingredients have precedents. The contribution here is the explicit realization of the stated families and finite examples, together with exact certificates.</p>
